Most B2B businesses I speak to are not short of website traffic. What they are short of is enquiries their sales team can actually quote on.

Over 13 years in B2B marketing, the same handful of problems come up again and again. None of them needs a full rebuild to start fixing. Here are the five I see most often, and the order I would tackle them in.

## 1. The website talks about you, not the buyer's problem

A typical homepage opens with company history, a mission statement or a stock photo of a handshake. A technical buyer arriving from a search wants to know three things, quickly:

- Do you do the thing I need?
- Can you do it to the standard I need?
- What do I do next?

If the first screen doesn't answer those, most visitors will go back to the search results. Lead with the problem you solve and who you solve it for. The history can come later.

## 2. There is no obvious next step

"Contact us" in the footer is not a next step. Every important page should end with one clear action that suits the visitor's stage:

- a request for a quote on a product or capability page
- a short call with an engineer or specialist on a technical page
- a datasheet or guide for someone who is still researching

One primary action per page, said in plain words, almost always beats a page of competing buttons.

## 3. The enquiry form asks for too much, or the wrong things

Long forms put people off. But so do forms that can't capture what your team needs to respond, such as a drawing, a quantity or a deadline. The result is a back-and-forth by email before anyone can quote.

Ask only for what you need to give a useful reply. Let people attach files where that is how your industry works, and tell them what happens after they press send.

## 4. You are visible for the wrong searches

Rankings and traffic are easy to report and easy to misread. A page that ranks well for a broad, informational term can bring in hundreds of visitors who were never going to buy.

The searches that matter are the ones that signal intent: a specific capability, a material, an application, a location. Build and improve the pages that answer those searches, even if the volumes look smaller.

## 5. Nobody can see where enquiries come from

This is the one that makes the other four hard to fix. If form submissions aren't tracked consistently, and your CRM doesn't record the source and landing page of each enquiry, you can't tell which pages, searches or campaigns are bringing in work.

You end up arguing about opinions instead of looking at evidence.

## What to fix first

If I had to choose an order, it would be this:

1. **Measurement.** Make sure every form and contact route is tracked, and every enquiry records where it came from. It is rarely glamorous, but everything else depends on it.
2. **The next step.** Give your most-visited pages a clear, specific action.
3. **The form.** Cut it to what you need, and make it work the way your buyers do.
4. **The message.** Rewrite the first screen of your key pages around the buyer's problem.
5. **Search visibility.** Focus SEO effort on the high-intent searches the first four fixes can now convert.

Then measure again, and decide what to change next. That loop of fixing, measuring and improving is what turns a website from a brochure into something that produces pipeline.
